Bringing a family together is one of the most frequent —and most human— reasons to begin an immigration process in Chile. The law allows residency to be obtained through a family bond with a Chilean national or with a foreign national who already holds residency. This is general guidance on where to start.

## Who does it apply to?

Broadly speaking, residency through a family bond may reach:

- Spouses and civil partners.
- Children.
- Parents.
- Other bonds recognized by the regulations, depending on the case.

The bond must be proven with official documentation, and specific requirements vary according to the relationship and each person's immigration situation.

## What is usually required

Although every procedure is different, the most common documents are:

- A valid ID or passport.
- Certificates proving the bond (marriage, birth, civil union).
- Records of the relative who resides in or is a national of Chile.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Letting deadlines lapse.** Many rights are lost simply by not acting in time.
2. **Submitting incomplete or unlegalized / non-apostilled documents.**
3. **Assuming a rejection is final.** Often there is a way to appeal.
