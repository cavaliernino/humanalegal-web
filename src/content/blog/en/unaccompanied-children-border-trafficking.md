## Why a child travelling without their parents is a red flag

Not every child who enters without their parents is a trafficking victim. But in child-protection terms, a **separated or unaccompanied** minor crossing a border is, by definition, a risk situation that requires verification. Human trafficking feeds precisely on those gaps: transfers no one examines, family ties no one confirms, children listed as being "in the care" of an adult whose relationship to them was never established.

It helps to distinguish two things that are often confused:

- **Migrant smuggling** is a crime against the State: someone profits from facilitating an irregular border crossing.
- **Human trafficking** is a crime against the person: there is recruitment, transfer and exploitation (sexual, labour, servitude, among others). When the victim is a child, deceit or coercion need not even be proven for the offence to exist.

A child arriving without their parents does not prove trafficking. But it does require ruling it out — and to rule it out, someone has to look. The problem is that, often, no one does.

## The standard that governs all of this: the best interests of the child

Above any migration logic stands a principle Chile bound itself to when it ratified the **Convention on the Rights of the Child**: the **best interests of the child**. That standard is not decorative. It means that, in case of doubt, the State's response cannot be "let them through and see what happens," but rather **protect first and always verify**.
